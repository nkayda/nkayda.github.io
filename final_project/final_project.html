Introduction

The SkyTrain

With several kinds of transportation methods available across Vancouver, transportation has played a massive role in city planning, construction of residential units, and shaping of communities. The Skytrain began serving residents of Vancouver in 1985 making it a little over 40 years old as of 2026.

Fun Fact!

The SkyTrain was prompted by the 1986 World Exposition on Transportation and Communication, known as Expo 86, during Vancouver decided to develop an automated rapid transit system in alignment with Expo 86’s theme of World in motion — World in touch

Our Experience

Even today the SkyTrain is an important part of everyday life for many people in Metro Vancouver, including us. As university students, the SkyTrain is an integral part of our lives, bringing us to and from classes and connecting us to one another.

We hope that residents feel more informed after viewing our visualizations by learning about, or increasing their awareness, of the socioeconomic and geographical factors impacting, and impacted by, trends in the development of public transportation.

SkyTrain Stations

SkyTrain stations over time (2001–2021)

Over the years, the Skytrain has expanded across the greater Vancouver Area, vastly increasing the quality of the city’s rapid transit system. Between 2001 and 2021, 39 Skytrain Stations were built to better serve Metro-Vancouver residents including the Millennium Line which serves as a key line for students of Simon Fraser University like us.

Key development moments

It’s easy to notice bunches of stations popping up over time. Some notable development projects include:

Millennium Line – 2002 to 2006

The Millennium Line began operation in 2002 but would see more stations being added until 2006, leading to a total of 12 stations.

Canada Line – 2009

15 stations were added as part of the Canada Line, adding 19.2 km to the SkyTrain network and a direct connection to Vancouver International Airport (YVR).

Evergreen Expansion – 2016

The Millennium Line was extended by 6 stations, adding 11 km to the SkyTrain network deeper into Coquitlam.

Fun Fact!

The Canada Line was developed in advance of the 2011 Olympics, for which Vancouver was the host city.

Between 2001–2021 the population of Metro Vancouver has risen from 1,990,000 to 2,606,000.

All cities in Metro-Vancouver saw consistent population growth from 2001-2021, although some areas experienced comparatively high growths. Surrey for example, saw increases of over 9% every 5 years with a peak in 2006-2011, while Port Moody saw a 17.2% increase in population from 2006-2011 alone – that’s nearly 5,300 people in 5 years, or more than 1,000 people each year!

According to TransLink, one of their main goals is to expand transit service into historically underserved communities to improve access and social equity.

Now, it is clear that SkyTrain stations overlap with lower-income areas, connecting underserved communities to schools, workplaces, and shopping centres.

Key findings

According to the 2022 Translink Customer Service Report, transit riders are split into two categories:

Captive Riders

Those who do not have regular access to a vehicle Usually employed part-time, less than $40k household income, aged 18-34, high frequency riders.

Choice Riders

Those who do have regular access to a vehicle. Usually employed full-time, ~$80k household income, aged 18-34, low frequency riders.
